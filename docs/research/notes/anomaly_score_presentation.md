# Presenting anomaly scores and detections to human analysts: ranking vs thresholding, risk-score aggregation, explainability, user-adjustable sensitivity

Context for all sections: target is a daily-batch internal dashboard where 1–3 analysts look for misuse in aggregate ChatGPT usage data (tokens, DAU per product per day). No per-user events. "Entity" in the SIEM sources below (user/host) maps to "product" (or product × day) in this setting; "alert" maps to "signal from one detector on one product-day".

## Key question 1: Risk-score aggregation — Splunk RBA, Microsoft Sentinel Fusion, Elastic entity risk, Exabeam risk timelines

### Takeaway
All four vendors converge on the same architecture: many low-fidelity detections each add a weighted score to a per-entity "bucket" over a time window, and the analyst is only notified when the aggregate crosses a line (Splunk: >100 points in 24 h, or ≥3 distinct ATT&CK tactics from ≥4 sources in 7 days; Exabeam: session score ≥90; Elastic: 0–100 score bucketed into five bands). Splunk's practitioner claim (vendor source) is a 50–90% reduction in alert volume with higher fidelity.

### Cited Findings

**Splunk Risk-Based Alerting (RBA)**
- Definition (vendor docs): RBA "is Splunk's method to aggregate low-fidelity security events as interesting observations tagged with security metadata to create high-fidelity, low-volume alerts." — [Splunk Community, Risk-Based Alerting FAQs](https://community.splunk.com/t5/Splunk-Enterprise-Security/Risk-Based-Alerting-FAQs/td-p/668170)
- Volume reduction claim (vendor marketing/practitioner blog): "Alert volume is regularly reduced by 50-90%, alert fidelity is vastly increased" — Haylee Mills, Splunk blog, 4 Apr 2022, [Risk-Based Alerting: The New Frontier for SIEM](https://www.splunk.com/en_us/blog/security/risk-based-alerting-the-new-frontier-for-siem.html). The same 50%–90% figure appears in the Splunk community FAQ — [Splunk Community FAQ](https://community.splunk.com/t5/Splunk-Enterprise-Security/Risk-Based-Alerting-FAQs/td-p/668170). Note: these are vendor-reported customer outcomes, not independently measured.
- Mental model (Mills, same blog): "I'm just storing anything that is interesting by that host, user, or ID and putting it into its own special bucket. I will create an alert only if there are enough interesting things in the bucket." — [Splunk blog, Mills 2022](https://www.splunk.com/en_us/blog/security/risk-based-alerting-the-new-frontier-for-siem.html)
- Score weighting by context (Mills): "I can also tweak the score using information about that object. So maybe this is a public facing production server or database, or this system has a known vulnerability, or this user's business unit definitely shouldn't be running these types of commands." — [Splunk blog, Mills 2022](https://www.splunk.com/en_us/blog/security/risk-based-alerting-the-new-frontier-for-siem.html)
- Mills' stated goals: "Reduce the number of overall alerts while increasing the fidelity of alerts that arise"; "One of the most impactful things RBA can do is remove busywork from your analyst queues"; five building blocks: "Build in Parallel", "Build Threat Object", "Build Rule Diversity", "Build Signature Based Sources", "Build Noisy Content into Risk" — [Splunk blog, The New Improved Splunk Guide to RBA](https://www.splunk.com/en_us/blog/security/the-new-improved-splunk-guide-to-risk-based-alerting.html)
- Default risk incident rules (primary docs, ES 7.3): "Risk Threshold Exceeded for Object Over 24 Hour Period" creates risk events "when the risk score for an object exceeds 100 over the last 24 hours" by summing all scores for the object ("if an object has eight related events, each with a calculated risk score, the search adds all the eight scores together"). "ATT&CK Tactic Threshold Exceeded for Object Over Previous 7 Days" fires when "the number of MITRE tactics exceeds 3 over the last 7 days" with `source_count >= 4` (distinct detection sources). Customization guidance: "increase the risk score threshold by 200 instead of 100" if appropriate for the environment. — [Splunk Docs, Default risk incident rules](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise-security-7/risk-based-alerting/7.3/identify-threat/default-risk-incident-rules-in-splunk-enterprise-security)
- ES 8 architecture: detections "create an intermediate finding which includes a score and metadata like its applicable MITRE ATT&CK technique and tactic"; another detection "aggregates these intermediate findings and creates a finding to investigate when they surpass thresholds." "The finding risk score is dependent on time frame." Risk badges show "Entities with risk events from the past seven days". Manual counterbalancing is supported: "you can manually create a risk entry with a risk score of -180". "A risk score is the single metric that shows the relative risk of an entity over time." — [Splunk Docs, Risk scoring in ES 8.7](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise-security-8/administer/8.7/risk-based-alerting)
- Stated benefit (vendor docs): RBA is claimed to "Improve the detection of sophisticated threats including low and slow attacks often missed by traditional SIEM products" and to let teams "reduce alert volume and focus only on findings that might represent a security threat." — [Splunk Community FAQ](https://community.splunk.com/t5/Splunk-Enterprise-Security/Risk-Based-Alerting-FAQs/td-p/668170)
- Haylee Mills' .conf 2020 talk "Streamlining Analysis of Security Stories with Risk-based Alerting" (SEC1113A) — [slides PDF](https://conf.splunk.com/files/2020/slides/SEC1113A.pdf) (not fetched; listed for the report writer).

**Microsoft Sentinel Fusion and customizable anomalies**
- Fusion is "a correlation engine based on scalable machine learning algorithms, to automatically detect multistage attacks ... by identifying combinations of anomalous behaviors and suspicious activities." Incidents "comprise two or more alerts or activities. By design, these incidents are low-volume, high-fidelity, and high-severity." Fusion detections "are presented as Fusion incidents on the Microsoft Sentinel Incidents page and not as alerts". Training window: "Microsoft Sentinel currently uses 30 days of historical data". Example: a ransomware incident is raised when alerts with severities informational/medium/informational/low/informational co-occur on one host in a timeframe. — [Microsoft Learn, Advanced multistage attack detection](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/sentinel/fusion)
- Sentinel's anomaly philosophy (directly applicable to weak signals): "A single anomaly is not a strong signal of malicious behavior, but a combination of several anomalies at different points on the kill chain sends a clear message." Anomalies "are notoriously noisy. They typically require a lot of tedious tuning". Tuning UX: "The thresholds and parameters for many of the anomalies can be configured and fine-tuned through the already familiar analytics rule user interface. The performance of the original threshold and parameters can be compared to the new ones within the interface and further tuned as necessary during a testing, or flighting, phase. Once the anomaly meets the performance objectives, the anomaly with the new threshold or parameters can be promoted to production with the click of a button." UEBA baselines: "Each entity's baseline behavior is set according to its own historical activities, those of its peers, and those of the organization as a whole." — [Microsoft Learn, Customizable anomalies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/sentinel/soc-ml-anomalies)

**Elastic Security entity risk scoring**
- "Alerts with higher risk scores contribute more than those with lower risk scores"; asset-criticality multiplier (Low 0.5, Medium 1, High 1.5, Extreme 2); "a single entity risk score of 0-100 is generated". Risk level bands: Unknown <20, Low 20–40, Moderate 40–70, High 70–90, Critical >90. "The risk scoring maintainer runs hourly to aggregate `Open` and `Acknowledged` alerts from the last 30 days." Residual risk: when all alerts close or age out, "the entity retains its last computed risk score until a new alert causes the score to be recalculated." The entity's risk summary separately shows the Alerts contribution and the Asset Criticality contribution (worked example: 36.16 + 16.95 = 53.11). — [Elastic Docs, Entity risk scoring](https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/security/advanced-entity-analytics/entity-risk-scoring); older version [8.19](https://www.elastic.co/guide/en/security/8.19/entity-risk-scoring.html)

**Exabeam Advanced Analytics (UEBA) risk timelines**
- "Notable Users" are users "who had a session score that met or exceeded the configured threshold, with a default of 90" (same for Notable Assets). — [Exabeam Docs, Advanced Analytics homepage](https://docs.exabeam.com/en/cloud-delivered-advanced-analytics/all/user-guide/153651-welcome-to-the-advanced-analytics-homepage.html)
- Per-event points: a rule's contribution is "the anchor score multiplied by the anomaly factor" (anchor from the triggered rule; anomaly factor measures deviation from the user's normal). User Risk Trend: "The y-axis shows the risk score out of 100"; colour coding "Green indicates that the session was not risky. Yellow indicates that the session was risky. Red indicates that the session was highly risky." Timeline shows "all the events in chronological order during the session" with "an orange up arrow indicates this is a risk increasing event, while the green down arrow indicates a risk reducing event." Trend windows: "one week, one month, three months, or one year". — [Exabeam Docs, Get to know a user profile](https://docs.exabeam.com/en/cloud-delivered-advanced-analytics/all/user-guide/153664-get-to-know-a-user-profile.html)
- Vendor description of Smart Timelines: they "show the full scope of user and device activity (including all related normal behavior) to quickly pinpoint anomalous behavior and risk"; "The system triggers an alert if the total risk score for multiple models exceeds a predetermined value." — [Exabeam blog, Smart Timelines (vendor marketing)](https://www.exabeam.com/blog/siem-trends/smart-timelines-nine-benefits-for-detecting-and-investigating-security-incidents/)

### Inferences
- Translation to the dashboard: treat each product (or product×day) as the "risk object"; each detector's graded signal is a "risk modifier" with points; keep a rolling 7-day (Splunk uses 24 h and 7 d windows) per-product risk total; surface a product in the "needs review" list only when (a) the sum exceeds a line, or (b) several *different* detectors fire (Splunk's "≥3 distinct tactics from ≥4 sources" is a diversity rule, which protects against one noisy detector dominating). Both rules can coexist, as in Splunk's defaults.
- Exabeam's "normal events shown in the same timeline, up/down arrows for score changes" and Elastic's "show contribution by category" both argue for displaying the score *decomposition* (which detectors added how many points) next to the total, not just the total.
- Negative modifiers (Splunk's -180 manual entry; Exabeam's risk-reducing events) are a precedent for letting analysts record "known cause / expected" to pull a product's score down without disabling detectors.
- Elastic's residual-risk rule (score persists until recalculated) and its 0–100 banding are a precedent for showing a stable per-product score that does not flicker daily.

### Gaps
- Elastic's exact aggregation formula (reported elsewhere as a Riemann-zeta-weighted sum of top alerts) is not stated on the documentation page fetched; only the qualitative "higher scores contribute more" and the worked example are documented. Treat the "zeta" detail as unverified.
- Splunk's ES 8 docs did not state numeric thresholds; the 100-point/24 h and 3-tactics/7-day rules are from ES 7.3 docs and may differ in newer versions.
- The 50–90% figure has no independent measurement; it is Splunk's own claim.
- Splunk documentation does not describe automatic score *decay* (only time-windowed sums); I found no primary source describing exponential decay in RBA.

## Key question 2: Explainable anomaly detection — which explanation forms help analysts

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced forms are (1) per-feature attribution split into "contributing" vs "offsetting" features (Antwarg et al.: in >85% of claims the top 1–3 features explained the anomaly to experts), and (2) attribution relative to the *nearest normal point*, whose values double as "expected values" (Sipple, ICML 2020). Practitioner studies (ALARM, with Capital One fraud analysts) found simple histograms/density plots were used most and complex projections were "too complex"; the survey literature notes there is still no consensus on evaluating explanations with humans.

### Cited Findings
- Motivation (Antwarg et al., 2019/2021): "Anomaly detection algorithms are often thought to be limited because they don't facilitate the process of validating results performed by domain experts." "An explanation of why an instance is anomalous enables the experts to focus their investigation on most important anomalies and may increase their trust in the algorithm." — [Antwarg, Miller, Shapira, Rokach, Explaining Anomalies Detected by Autoencoders Using SHAP, arXiv 1903.02407](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.02407)
- Explanation form (Antwarg et al.): the method explains the features with highest reconstruction error that account for an adjustable share of total error (80% in their output), and for each, lists the input features that "contribute" to the anomaly (red) vs "offset" it (blue), colour intensity = SHAP magnitude. User study: four domain experts reviewed 114 top anomalies from "15,000 warranty claims from a large car manufacturer with 1,000 features per instance"; experts "reported that the explanations provided a clear indication of how to examine the anomalies" and "in over 85 percent of the claims, the first one to three explanatory features explained why the claim was anomalous." Experts "took a day...to adapt to working with the visual depiction tool, but after that, they were comfortable with these visualizations and satisfied with the explanations", especially for "complicated claims." — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.02407)
- Sipple (ICML 2020), motivation: "To aid diagnosis and defect remediation, it is often desirable, especially with high-dimensional data, to provide additional information to help understanding the anomaly along with the anomaly score." "The anomaly detector should therefore attribute a proportional blame to those variables that contributed to the anomaly score." Baseline choice: "the baseline point used by Integrated Gradients for comparison is the nearest normal point observed in the historical training set." Presentation to operators: "Along with the proportional blame provided by Integrated Gradients, we have found that the values of u* provide useful interpretation as expected values of the nearest normal." Diagnostic value of attribution breadth: "Highlighting a single anomalous dimension usually identifies a broken sensor, whereas identifying multiple dimensions helps pinpoint a mechanical failure caused by a defective valve, stuck damper, etc." Deployed on "over 15,000 climate-control and power meter devices in 145 Google office buildings". — [Sipple, Interpretable, Multidimensional, Multimodal Anomaly Detection with Negative Sampling for Detection of Device Failure, ICML 2020 / arXiv 2007.10088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10088); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.10088); code: [google/madi](https://github.com/google/madi)
- Survey taxonomy (Li, Zhu, van Leeuwen, ACM TKDD 18(1), 2023): explanation methods are "feature-based" ("indicate which features are important and/or the corresponding values of investigated anomalies"), "sample-based" ("compares the abnormal object directly to normal objects to demonstrate differences"), or both; eight technique families (approximation-based e.g. LIME/Anchors, perturbation, reconstruction-error, attention, gradient, causal, visualization, intrinsically explainable). On human evaluation: "there is no consensus on what a good XAD technique should be" and "evaluation of XAD techniques is largely dependent on the application domain and end-users"; questions like "how do humans understand an explanation" are explicitly out of scope. — [A Survey on Explainable Anomaly Detection, arXiv 2210.06959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06959v2)
- ALARM (Analyst-in-the-Loop Anomaly Reasoning and Management; Capital One / Akoglu group, 2023): explanations are per-anomaly feature importance weights reflecting "subspaces in which the anomalies stand out the most"; GUI has a Summary View (anomalies clustered, colour-coded by score), Exploration View (histograms, density plots, parallel coordinates, auto-selected feature pairs), Rule Candidates View ("up to three data-driven rules" filtered by coverage/purity), and a Rule Design Interface with sliders and real-time coverage/purity. Study with three Capital One fraud analysts (card fraud, bank fraud, AML): "Average time-to-rule on Czech using ALARM (around 6 mins) is shorter than that the analysts spent using ad-hoc tools (8 mins)"; analysts "consistently took most advantage of the simple histogram and density plots," while MDS projections and Lookout were "too complex to get into"; the iterative rule interface was "a massive value proposition as compared to doing this manually"; overall "a large time saver". — [From Explanation to Action: An End-to-End Human-in-the-loop Framework for Anomaly Reasoning and Management, arXiv 2304.03368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03368); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03368)
- Human-in-the-loop detection premise (Das/Doppa line of work): "Anomaly detection systems are not practical when the number of false-positives is very high resulting in wasted human effort, so anomaly detectors must be configurable by human analysts to minimize effort on false positives"; GLAD "learns their local relevance to specific data instances using label feedback from human analysts." — [GLAD: GLocalized Anomaly Detection via Human-in-the-Loop Learning, arXiv 1810.01403](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.01403)
- Azure Anomaly Detector (retiring 1 Oct 2026) returns, per point, "each data point's expected value, and the upper and lower anomaly detection boundaries. You can use these values to visualize the range of normal values, and anomalies in the data." Boundaries: `upperBoundary = expectedValue + (100 - marginScale) * upperMargin`, `lowerBoundary = expectedValue - (100 - marginScale) * lowerMargin`. — [Microsoft Learn, Anomaly Detector how-to](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/anomaly-detector/how-to/identify-anomalies)

### Inferences
- For aggregate product/day data with few dimensions (tokens, DAU, tokens-per-DAU, per-product share), the "nearest normal / expected value" form (Sipple) is the natural explanation: show observed vs expected (same-day-kind baseline) with the band, and name the one or two metrics that carry the deviation. Sipple's single-vs-multiple-dimension heuristic maps to: one metric off → likely data/instrumentation glitch; several metrics off together → likely a real behaviour change.
- Antwarg's "contributing vs offsetting" split suggests showing not only what raised the score but what argues *against* an anomaly (e.g. "tokens +180% but DAU also +150%, so tokens/DAU is normal"), which filters false positives faster.
- ALARM's finding that analysts gravitated to simple histograms/density plots over sophisticated projections supports a dashboard built from plain time series with bands and sorted tables rather than 2-D embeddings.
- The survey's explicit caveat (no consensus on human evaluation) means the dashboard team should run its own lightweight check with the 1–3 analysts (e.g. do the top-3 reasons explain the flag in >85% of cases, mirroring Antwarg's metric).

### Gaps
- No HCI study specifically on analyst *trust* in anomaly explanations with measured trust scales was found in this pass; Antwarg's evidence is qualitative satisfaction plus the 85% coverage figure. "LIME/SHAP for anomaly detection" HCI evaluations beyond Antwarg were not located.
- Microsoft "Anomaly Detector" UX guidance beyond the API's expected-value/boundary design was not found; the service is being retired.

## Key question 3: Ranking vs thresholding — precision@k, review budgets, and sensitivity controls

### Takeaway
Industrial and research sources frame triage as a fixed-capacity ranking problem: evaluate and present by precision@k where k matches the analyst's daily review budget (Freitas et al. 2026: K∈{5,10,20}, "set-oriented: analysts need the right incidents near the top of the queue"), with a bounded display score alongside full-resolution ordering. Sensitivity/threshold sliders exist in products (Azure Anomaly Detector 0–100, Sentinel flighting) but the only interface study found (PreCall) is a prototype with no completed user study.

### Cited Findings
- Freitas, Gharib, Magenheim (Microsoft, 2026), Adaptive Incident Prioritization (AIP): "Large security operations centers (SOCs) often face hundreds of active incidents per day, creating substantial cognitive and operational load for analysts"; prior work cited: "large organizations can see up to 232 incidents per day, 51% of which are never resolved." Metric choice: "We evaluate K∈{5,10,20} to capture the region where analyst attention is most concentrated." Product framing: "This product experience is set-oriented: analysts need the right incidents near the top of the queue, while the exact ordering within that visible set is less important." Display: a "bounded display score Dt(i) capped at 100" while retaining "full ordering resolution of the model". Results: "92.8% P@10" on expert-reviewed evaluation across 1,000 organizations; "5.8% relative lift" in alert-detail interaction vs severity ordering; deployed "across tens of thousands of customers" in Microsoft Defender's Queue Assistant. — [arXiv 2607.16963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16963)
- Fraud/AML queue framing: "Investigation teams have fixed analyst capacity; a scored queue determines which accounts receive review and which do not"; queue construction "is a decision-support task: the system does not directly decide guilt or innocence but structures the analyst's workload". — [Do Transaction-Level and Actor-Level AML Queues Agree?, arXiv 2604.23494](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23494)
- Warranty-fraud practitioner case (vendor case study): without ranking "reviewers scan a first-in-first-out queue", with ranking "the same queue is ranked by fraud likelihood with the supporting signals attached." — [Spread.ai customer page (vendor marketing)](https://www.spread.ai/customers/warranty-claims-fraud-ranking?lang=de)
- Evaluation of unsupervised outlier rankings: Campos et al. 2016 (Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery 30(4):891–927) examine "the most commonly-used measures for comparing the performance of different methods, and suggest adaptations that are more suitable for the evaluation of outlier detection results"; their supplementary page also uses "maximum F-measure ... over all possible outlier score thresholds". — [LMU supplementary page](https://www.dbs.ifi.lmu.de/research/outlier-evaluation/); [paper record](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/47435)
- Azure Anomaly Detector sensitivity: "a numerical value to adjust the tolerance of the anomaly detection" in range (0, 100]; "When increased, the expected value range will be tighter, and more anomalies will be reported. When turned down, the expected value range will be wider, and fewer anomalies will be reported." The docs illustrate the same series at sensitivity 99, 95 and 85 to show the band widening. — [Microsoft Learn, identify anomalies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/anomaly-detector/how-to/identify-anomalies) (sensitivity semantics also summarised in [docs.azure.cn mirror](https://docs.azure.cn/en-us/ai-services/anomaly-detector/how-to/identify-anomalies))
- Sentinel tuning workflow (compare old vs new threshold in a flighting phase, then promote) — see Q1, [Microsoft Learn, Customizable anomalies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/sentinel/soc-ml-anomalies)
- PreCall (Wikimedia ORES, 2019 workshop paper): "a slider next to the radar chart represents the threshold which determines if an edit is declared as good or damaging"; "a color gradient illustrates the fact that the transition from good to damaging edits is fluid, i.e., there is a range of uncertainty"; changing the slider immediately updates precision/recall/FPR. The authors only "envision a qualitative user study" and flag "a particular concern is the level of abstraction PreCall should provide, such as whether our inclusion of measures like precision and recall is interpretable". — [arXiv 1907.05131](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05131); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.05131)
- Splunk's threshold-customisation guidance (raise 100 → 200 if needed) is a per-environment knob rather than a per-session slider — [Splunk Docs](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise-security-7/risk-based-alerting/7.3/identify-threat/default-risk-incident-rules-in-splunk-enterprise-security)

### Inferences
- For 1–3 analysts reviewing once per day, the review budget is small (plausibly 5–10 product-days). Presenting a ranked list truncated to that k, plus a bounded 0–100 display score, follows the AIP design; the fixed threshold then becomes a soft marker ("above the line") rather than the gate on what is shown.
- A sensitivity control should be expressed in the Azure/Sentinel way — as band width ("how far from expected before we flag") with an immediate preview of how many product-days would be flagged under the new setting over the last N days — rather than as an abstract precision/recall dial (PreCall's own concern about interpretability of P/R for non-ML users).
- Because the dashboard has no labels, precision@k cannot be computed automatically; analysts' dispositions ("real misuse / explainable / noise") on the top-k each day are the cheapest way to obtain it and to calibrate the sensitivity default.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found that measures how analysts calibrate a sensitivity slider or how tunable thresholds change trust; PreCall has no completed user study and the search snippet claiming "a smaller percentage of users used the sliders than expected" could not be traced to the PreCall text (it may come from a different paper) — treat as unverified.
- I did not locate fraud-ops practitioner writeups with concrete "top N per day" review-budget numbers from a primary source; the AML queue paper and the Microsoft AIP paper are the strongest substitutes.

## Key question 4: Score presentation — numbers vs bands vs ranks; what PAIR and HAX say for a sensitivity control and signal explanations

### Takeaway
Google's PAIR guidebook warns that numeric confidence "is risky" and recommends categorical buckets or N-best alternatives unless the number changes a decision; Microsoft's HAX guidelines G2 (make clear how well the system performs), G11 (explain why), G16 (convey consequences of user actions) and G17 (global controls) map directly onto a sensitivity control with a preview of its effect and per-signal explanations. Vendors in Q1 all display a 0–100 score *and* a band (Elastic five bands; Exabeam green/yellow/red).

### Cited Findings
- PAIR, Explainability + Trust: "If it doesn't make an impact on user decision making, consider not showing it." "If the confidence level could be misleading for less-savvy users, reconsider how it's displayed, or whether to display it at all." Categorical: "These visualizations categorize confidence values into buckets, such as High / Medium / Low and show the category rather than the numerical value." N-best: "Rather than providing an explicit indicator of confidence, the system can display the N-best alternative results". Numeric: "Numeric confidence indicators are risky because they presume your users have a good baseline understanding of probability." Data transparency: "Telling the user what data are being used in the AI's prediction can help your product avoid contextual surprises and privacy suspicion and help the user know when to apply their own judgment." Cause-effect: "Showing clear cause-effect relationships between user actions and system outputs with explanations can help users develop the right level of trust over time." — [People + AI Guidebook, Explainability + Trust](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)
- PAIR also advises teams to "brainstorm what kinds of interactions, results, and explanations would decrease, maintain, or inflate trust along a spectrum from 'No trust' to 'Too much trust'" and to "consider changing how results are presented based on different confidence thresholds" (codelab summary of the guidebook). — [Google Codelab, Building Trusted AI Products with the PAIR Guidebook](https://codelabs.developers.google.com/codelabs/pair-guidebook)
- Microsoft HAX Guidelines (18): G1 "Make clear what the system can do."; G2 "Make clear how well the system can do what it can do." — "Help the user understand how often the AI system may make mistakes."; G4 "Show contextually relevant information."; G8 "Support efficient dismissal."; G9 "Support efficient correction."; G10 "Scope services when in doubt."; G11 "Make clear why the system did what it did." — "Enable the user to access an explanation of why the AI system behaved."; G14 "Update and adapt cautiously."; G15 "Encourage granular feedback." — "Enable the user to provide feedback indicating their preferences during interaction."; G16 "Convey the consequences of user actions." — "Immediately update or convey how user actions will impact future behaviors."; G17 "Provide global controls." — "Allow the user to globally customize what the AI system monitors and how it behaves."; G18 "Notify users about changes." — [Microsoft HAX Design Library](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/library/)
- Vendor practice: Elastic shows a 0–100 score plus Unknown/Low/Moderate/High/Critical bands and the per-category contributions — [Elastic Docs](https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/security/advanced-entity-analytics/entity-risk-scoring); Exabeam shows score out of 100 plus green/yellow/red session colouring and per-event up/down arrows — [Exabeam Docs](https://docs.exabeam.com/en/cloud-delivered-advanced-analytics/all/user-guide/153664-get-to-know-a-user-profile.html); Microsoft AIP shows a display score capped at 100 while ranking at full resolution — [arXiv 2607.16963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16963); Splunk ES shows risk badges for entities with risk in the past seven days — [Splunk Docs](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise-security-8/administer/8.7/risk-based-alerting)

### Inferences
- Combined pattern from PAIR + vendors: lead with rank and band (e.g. "Review / Watch / Normal"), keep the numeric score secondary (tooltip or small text) for tie-breaking, and avoid probability-looking numbers (e.g. "87% anomalous") that the 1–3 analysts have no basis to calibrate.
- A sensitivity control satisfies G17 only if paired with G16: show, on change, how many product-days of the last 30 would move across the line, and G2: state the expected false-flag rate at that setting (e.g. "at this setting roughly X of the last 30 days would have been flagged").
- G11/G15 imply each flagged product-day should carry a one-line "why" (detector names, observed vs expected) and a disposition control whose effect on future baselines is stated explicitly (G16), mirroring Splunk's negative risk entries.

### Gaps
- The PAIR guidebook does not specifically address anomaly scores or sensitivity sliders; the guidance is on model confidence in general and had to be transferred.
- HAX pattern-level guidance for G17 in monitoring/alerting products (as opposed to Outlook/Copilot examples) was not found.

## Key question 5: Temporal presentation — "what changed since yesterday", new vs persisting states

### Takeaway
Monitoring tools expose explicit state machines (Grafana Normal/Pending/Alerting plus No Data/Error; Datadog OK/Warn/Alert/No Data with a status-over-time graph and event stream), use a pending/hold period to suppress one-off blips, and record each transition as an event so analysts see "newly triggered" vs "still firing" vs "recovered". UEBA tools add a per-entity risk trend over weeks to months. Elastic's residual-risk rule keeps a score visible after its contributing alerts age out.

### Cited Findings
- Grafana alert instance states: Normal (condition not met), Pending ("breached the threshold but for less than the pending period"), Alerting ("breached the threshold for longer than the pending period"), plus NoData ("occurs when the alert rule query runs successfully but returns no data points at all") and Error ("triggered when the alert rule fails to evaluate its query or queries successfully"). "Alerts transition first to pending and then firing, thus it takes at least two evaluation cycles before an alert is fired." No Data and Error "honor the pending period: the alert instance transitions `Normal → Pending → No Data`"; "Set the pending period to `0` to skip the Pending state". — [Grafana Docs v12, State and health of alerts](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/v12.0/alerting/fundamentals/alert-rules/state-and-health); [latest](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/alerting/fundamentals/alert-rules/state-and-health/)
- Datadog monitor status: states "Alert, Warn, No Data, or OK"; "The status graph shows your monitor's status over time, broken out by group."; "Events generated from your monitor (alerts, warnings, recoveries, etc.) are shown in this section"; manual resolve "is artificially switching the monitor status to OK for its next evaluation" (ALERT -> OK -> ALERT if the condition persists); muting by scope is supported. — [Datadog Docs, Monitor status](https://docs.datadoghq.com/monitors/monitor_status/)
- Elastic residual risk: "the entity retains its last computed risk score until a new alert causes the score to be recalculated." — [Elastic Docs](https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/security/advanced-entity-analytics/entity-risk-scoring)
- Exabeam: User Risk Trend over "one week, one month, three months, or one year"; session timeline with risk-increasing/decreasing arrows. — [Exabeam Docs](https://docs.exabeam.com/en/cloud-delivered-advanced-analytics/all/user-guide/153664-get-to-know-a-user-profile.html)
- Splunk: risk badges show "Entities with risk events from the past seven days" — [Splunk Docs](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise-security-8/administer/8.7/risk-based-alerting); the 24 h and 7-day windows in default rules — [Splunk Docs](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise-security-7/risk-based-alerting/7.3/identify-threat/default-risk-incident-rules-in-splunk-enterprise-security)
- HAX G18 "Notify users about changes." and G12 "Remember recent interactions." — [Microsoft HAX Design Library](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/library/)

### Inferences
- For a daily batch, a per-product state of New (first day over the line) / Ongoing (n-th consecutive day) / Recovered (below the line after being over) / No data mirrors Grafana/Datadog and lets a 1–3-person team see at a glance what is actually new since yesterday. A one-day "pending" analogue (show "Watch" on day one, "Review" on day two) implements Grafana's two-evaluation rule for noisy detectors while still letting the ranked list show day-one signals.
- Per-product 7-day risk sparklines (Exabeam trend, Splunk 7-day badge) plus a transition log ("entered Review on 2026-09-29; still over the line 4 days") are the minimal temporal context.
- Datadog's "resolve forces OK then re-alerts if condition persists" is a warning: an analyst's "dismiss" should be recorded as a disposition with a reason, not as a state reset, otherwise the same product reappears tomorrow with no memory (HAX G12).

### Gaps
- Grafana's "Recovering" / "keep firing for" semantics could not be confirmed from the page fetched (the doc excerpt only covered No Data/Error); treat the Recovering state as unverified in this pass.
- No academic study on "novelty highlighting" or "new vs persisting anomaly" presentation for analysts was found; the evidence here is product documentation only.
